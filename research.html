Research

Understanding the influences that shape the use of physical activity spaces.

My research examines how social, environmental, historical, and structural conditions influence opportunities to engage with physical activity spaces in historically marginalized communities and how communities experience these spaces designed to support movement, recreation, and health.

Research Focus

My work is situated at the intersection of kinesiology and public health with a focus on equity.

I am particularly interested in understanding why physical activity opportunities may exist spatially but remain functionally inaccessible, undesirable, unsafe, underutilized, or disconnected from the communities they are intended to serve.

This perspective moves beyond simply asking whether parks, recreation centers, trails, courts, or other resources are present. Instead, my research examines how history, perception, infrastructure, social conditions, quality, safety, transportation, and lived experience influence whether those spaces become meaningful opportunities for participation.

Doctoral Research

Factors Influencing Use of Physical Spaces In Formerly Redlined Neighborhoods

Examining the social, environmental, and place-based factors that influence how residents experience and use physical activity spaces.

My doctoral research explores how historical patterns of neighborhood disinvestment and exclusion may continue to influence physical activity environments and experiences within communities today.

The study focuses not only on spatial access to physical activity resources, but also on what influences whether residents actually view those spaces as usable, accessible, welcoming, safe, and relevant to their lives.

Why This Research Matters

Communities can appear well served when access is measured only by the presence of parks, recreation facilities, trails, courts, or other physical activity infrastructure. That measure can overlook whether those resources are actually usable or meaningful to residents.

A more complete understanding of access can help practitioners, researchers, planners, recreation agencies, and policymakers identify barriers that traditional spatial measures may miss.

  • Improve how physical activity access is evaluated.
  • Better incorporate community experience into planning and program development.
  • Identify barriers related to safety, quality, infrastructure, transportation, and perception.
  • Support more equitable investment in recreation and physical activity environments.
  • Connect research findings to practical decisions within communities and public agencies.

Research should inform better environments and better decisions.

My goal is to connect scholarship with practical applications in physical activity, recreation, community programming, planning, and organizational decision-making.
