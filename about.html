About

A multidisciplinary career built around people, programs, and physical activity.

My professional path has crossed kinesiology, education, athletics, research, technology, business, and community programming. I have always believed in keeping doors to opportunity open, and over time I have found ways to connect those different disciplines rather than keep them separate. A lot of my work has come from seeing the gaps between fields, people, and programs—and finding ways to bridge those gaps, bring ideas together, and build something more complete.

Professional Background

I am a kinesiology professional, educator, researcher, coach, and program leader, but most of those identities have grown together rather than separately. I began coaching in 2011 and never really stopped. What started on the court eventually became a much broader career built around teaching, leadership, physical activity, and creating opportunities for other people to grow.

Education became another major part of that path. I spent nearly a decade working as a teacher, while continuing to coach and take on greater responsibilities in athletics. Those years gave me the opportunity to work closely with students and athletes, but they also pushed me to think more deeply about how programs are built, how people are supported, and how leadership can shape the environments people experience every day.

Over time, my work expanded into athletic administration, collegiate and community-based sport, program development, research, technology, and business. I have moved between roles that were very hands-on—teaching, coaching, mentoring, and running events—and roles that required me to step back and think about systems, operations, data, access, and long-term program development.

I have never viewed those experiences as separate careers. I tend to notice the spaces between disciplines and the gaps that exist between people, programs, and opportunities. Much of my work has been about finding ways to connect those pieces—bringing together sport, education, research, technology, and community programming to build something more complete and, hopefully, more useful to the people it serves.
